---
title: Crockford's Base32
description: Base32 for people without I and L and O and U. Decoding forgives look-alikes and hyphens
---

::encoding-facts{name="base32-crockford"}
::

Douglas Crockford's take on base32. No `I`, `L`, `O` or `U`. Nothing reads as something else. And nothing spells a rude word by accident, which is why `U` had to go. ULIDs use this alphabet.

```ts
encode("base32-crockford", "foobar");  // "CSQPYRK1E8"
decode("base32-crockford", "csqp-yrki-e8");
// the same six bytes: lowercase, hyphens, and i read as 1
```

Decoding ignores case and hyphens. It reads `O` as `0` and `I` or `L` as `1`. Lenient? No, that's the spec. This is the byte encoding. Crockford also defines a number encoding with a check symbol, which isn't here.
